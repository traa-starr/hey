# trigger check: where lessons leaned heavy, and what changed

October 2026. Every shipped lesson, the ten habit lessons, the ways to answer and the catch-it ideas were read start to finish, asking two questions:

1. Does this ask someone to sit in a hard feeling longer than it needs to?
2. Could something heavy end up kept in the book, or shown back to them later?

The rule that came out of it now lives in `CLAUDE.md` and `content/README.md`: **the book keeps kind things.** A lesson can meet a hard moment briefly, with one example, then turns toward something kinder. A test (`content.test.ts`) checks that no idea offered for keeping carries a heavy word.

## what was found, and how it was turned

| where | what it did | what it does now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| turn down the volume | kept "how you are, with a volume" in the book, with ideas like "a little tired." and "kind of nervous about tomorrow."; this is where sentences like "i'm sad and a little relieved" came from | the feeling question offers good and calm first; the kept sentence is one good feeling, with its volume ("i'm quietly content.") |
| a thought is not a fact | kept "i'm noticing the thought that they're annoyed with me." in the book | the frame is still tried on a common thought, but it isn't kept; a second step keeps one kinder thought to sit beside it |
| three kind lines | kept "this is hard…" as the line for the book | the kept line is the kind one: "when it's hard, i can give myself rest." |
| where it comes from | "some weren't" trying to help; "a parent's warning… the feed's opinion"; kept where the voice's words come from (family, school, work); the experiment listened for a word the voice uses a lot | most voices were trying to help, and there's room for a kinder one; keeps whose kind words still come back; the experiment listens for a kind word the voice already uses |
| habit lessons (all ten) | showed three inner-voice lines in a row before saying what the habit is trying to do, then kept an answer that often quoted the harsh line back | one example line is enough to recognize it; what's kept is one kind line from the habit's new `kind` list |
| the friend test | "probably something harsh" was answered with "the gap between those two voices is what we're working with" | "and the voice used for a friend is already in there, ready to turn inward." |
| the "you" trick | offered "are tired, and that makes sense." to keep | "can take this one step at a time." |
| savor it | opened with "minds are quick to replay what went badly" | opens with "here's a small skill for the good moments." |
| why to what | opened on "why am i like this?" and had people try on "why do i always ruin everything?" | a warm opener first; the try-on pairs a mild "why" with a concrete next step |
| everyone has one, disagreeing kindly, small wants, asking for it | small heavy edges in replies and labels | softened in place, same meaning |
| catch it ideas | "nobody really cares." | "i'm behind on everything." |

## what stayed, on purpose

- **A single inner-voice line, shown once.** Recognizing a habit needs one example. It is never kept unless the person opts in, and never resurfaced on home.
- **"it makes sense this is hard."** as a kind line. It meets a hard day with warmth instead of naming a harsh thought.
- **Real help in settings**, and the skip on "that's a hard one" in where it comes from.

## added alongside

- **Background cards** (`know`): every lesson now has a short break mid-lesson with where the idea comes from, each with its source and an honest evidence label. Every citation was checked against its paper before it went in.
- **Look backs** (`recall`): each later lesson opens with the idea from the one before, and home brings a finished lesson's idea back after 1, 3, 7 and 21 days, at most once a day. Trying to remember, spaced over days, helps ideas stick (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006; Cepeda et al., 2006). Nothing is graded, and "show me" is as welcome as "i remember it".
